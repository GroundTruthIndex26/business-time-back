# Business Time Back Rebuild Brief

## Conversion strategy

The rebuilt page will direct the first-time visitor through **one primary path: Explore the live demo**. Returning-user sign-in remains available as a quiet navigation link. The hero will identify the priority buyer as **operations leaders at growing service teams** and will carry a short outcome-led promise: **“Give your operations team hours back every week.”**

The page will demonstrate the product rather than merely describe it. A product-style opportunity map will show how work is categorized, how recoverable time is identified, and how one workflow change becomes a focused plan. The page will repeat the non-surveillance stance in practical language: it uses self-reported estimates, not activity monitoring, keystroke logging, or screen tracking.

Existing aggregate outcome claims from the original page—**11.6k hours reclaimed** and **41% average admin and meeting time**—will be retained as product context. Because no verified customer testimonials, customer logos, ratings, or price points were supplied, none will be fabricated. The page instead includes an honest proof framework, a visible FAQ, and a designated commercial-clarity panel whose exact pricing or trial language must be approved before a public release.

## Rebuild design directions

**Very Brief Intro:** A sunlit, human-centered operations tool that uses soft paper, teal, and warm orange to make workload visibility feel calm and optimistic rather than clinical.

**Probability:** 0.06

### Option 2: Dispatch Room

**Very Brief Intro:** A structured workboard inspired by operations planning, with sharp utility typography, signal colors, and an expressive data surface that makes reclaiming time feel actionable.

**Very Brief Intro:** A tactile editorial software aesthetic where airy visual systems, understated numbers, and a confident product canvas turn the abstract idea of capacity into something the visitor can see.

**Probability:** 0.04

## Chosen direction: Better Work, Visible

### Design Movement

Human-centered enterprise software through a contemporary editorial lens. The reference point is thoughtful workplace publishing, not surveillance software, productivity dashboards, or generic SaaS gradients.

### Core Principles

1. **Capacity is human.** Every visual choice should make recovered time feel like focus, judgment, and meaningful work—not headcount reduction.
2. **Show the logic.** Let the product canvas, workflow diagram, and directional numbers do explanatory work before long text does.
3. **Lead with one decision.** The primary CTA remains the visual anchor; secondary information stays quiet but discoverable.
4. **Earn trust in plain language.** State what the tool does and does not do concretely, avoid unsupported promises, and never simulate customer praise.

### Color Philosophy

Soft bone paper makes the product feel humane and legible. Deep ink gives the structure authority. A clear mineral teal signals progress and recovered capacity; a small tangerine accent marks moments of waste or friction. The palette has daylight warmth, never urgency-red or surveillance-blue.

### Layout Paradigm

An asymmetric guided walkthrough. Each section acts like a field station: a short label rail names the decision, the central narrative explains it, and an off-axis product canvas visualizes it. On mobile, the product canvas always follows its corresponding promise.

### Signature Elements

1. A large abstract daylight workspace image in the hero, with deliberate light contrast and textual safe area.
2. A circular “capacity loop” motif repeated as a small line mark, progress shape, and favicon.
3. Teal underlines and tangerine waste markers that visually distinguish time recovered from time absorbed by busywork.

### Interaction Philosophy

The page should feel like an approachable product tour. Buttons have clear active states; the product canvas responds to hover and focus with lightweight data emphasis. Motion is purposeful and never functions as a distraction.

### Animation

On load, hero content rises 12px while fading in over 420ms; the capacity loop draws once over 800ms. Dashboard bars and workflow connectors enter in a short 50ms stagger. Hover states use 160–200ms ease-out transitions. All nonessential motion is disabled for reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System

**DM Serif Display** carries major promises and question-led section titles. **Manrope** covers body copy, utility navigation, and buttons. **IBM Plex Mono** signals product labels, process steps, and metric captions. Display type is spacious and confident, while interface labels are compact and deliberately tracked.

### Brand Essence

**Business Time Back shows operations leaders where everyday work absorbs their team’s time—and helps them choose one better way forward.**

**Personality:** humane, clear-eyed, focused.

### Brand Voice

Use calm statements that put the user’s work first. Headlines name a practical gain; CTAs invite an informed next step; microcopy explains limitations without hedging.

Example headline: “See the work that is getting in the way of the work.”

Example CTA: “Explore the live demo.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The product mark is an open capacity loop: two offset arcs joining around a small forward point, suggesting hours released back into useful work. It uses no text and remains legible at favicon size.
